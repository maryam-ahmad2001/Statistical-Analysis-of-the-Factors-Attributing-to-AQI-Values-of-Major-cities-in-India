# Statistical-Analysis-of-the-Factors-Attributing-to-AQI-Values-of-Major-cities-in-India

***Overview***

Prompted by the severe smog events in Delhi in 2021, this group project investigated which factors most significantly affect the Air Quality Index (AQI) across major Indian cities. The study employed ANOVA-based hypothesis testing to identify statistically significant predictors of air quality degradation, and included a questionnaire component to understand citizen preferences for city characteristics.

***Motivation***

Delhi's air quality crisis made headlines globally and raised urgent questions about the drivers of urban air pollution in India. This project used that real-world event as its entry point, applying formal statistical methods to determine whether factors like vehicular traffic, rainfall, temperature, and industrial activity had a significant effect on AQI values.

***Methodology***

The analysis was structured across three phases:

**Phase 1 — Data Collection**
•	Secondary data on AQI values, rainfall, and temperature was gathered for major Indian cities
•	A City Choice Questionnaire was designed and distributed to capture citizen-level perceptions and preferences
•	Questionnaire responses were compiled and cleaned in Microsoft Excel

**Phase 2 — Statistical Testing**
•	One-way and multi-factor ANOVA was applied to test whether differences in AQI across cities and conditions were statistically significant
•	Hypothesis testing was structured around null hypotheses of no difference between group means
•	Regression analysis was used to identify which environmental variables most strongly predicted AQI levels

**Phase 3 — Presentation & Reporting**
•	Results were compiled into a PowerPoint presentation and an Excel workbook with embedded ANOVA outputs
•	Findings were interpreted in the context of urban planning and pollution policy implications

***Tools & Technologies***
•	Microsoft Excel (with ANOVA macros) — statistical analysis
•	Primary survey questionnaire — citizen data collection
•	PowerPoint — results presentation

***Key Takeaways***
The project demonstrated that certain environmental and infrastructural factors were statistically significant predictors of AQI, while others were not — a finding with direct implications for targeted policy interventions. The study also reinforced the value of combining primary and secondary data sources in environmental research.
